Stool math

Bar stools bought by eye end up too tall for the counter, too many for the row, or dangling feet with no footrest. Stool math turns the counter height into the seat range that fits under it, turns the counter length into the number of stools that fit along it, and turns the seat height into the footrest below it. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Height plan - the counter height minus a labeled knee gap becomes the seat range to shop for.
  • Space plan - the counter length at a labeled width a stool becomes the row count and the spare centimeters.
  • Footrest plan - the seat height minus a labeled drop becomes the footrest height.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: a knee gap of 25-30 cm below the counter, 60 cm of counter for each stool, a footrest 45 cm below the seat - all illustrative defaults you can change. Bodies, counters and stools all vary, and no arithmetic can tell you a seat is comfortable. This is planning arithmetic, not a fitting: sit on the real stool before buying a row.
